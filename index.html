How Deep Does a Next-Concept Model Need to Run?

Measuring the Depth Ceiling of Coarse-to-Fine Text Generation

Next-concept language models synthesize sequences through a fixed ladder of K coarse-to-fine parallel passes. In this research, we formulate the oracle depth ceiling: the exact proportion of passes an optimal selector with perfect foresight can safely omit at parity quality. Across benchmarks, we measure a 37.7% redundant pass ceiling on XSum summarisation (replicating at 39.3% and 37.6% across validation splits). We identify the meter slope phenomenon where the terminal pass leaves token sequence length unchanged while substituting approximately 1 in 7 lexical tokens, and design an unsupervised entity saturation early-exit criterion capturing one-third of the theoretical ceiling with negligible ROUGE-L divergence.
